<span style="color:white">You don't need to know much about hardware to start. A <span style="color:#ff2777">**cyberdeck**</span> can be as simple as a screen and a keyboard plugged into a small computer, or as complex as a fully custom portable machine.</span>

<br>

<img src="../assets_lab/cyberdeck.png" alt="cyberdeck illustration" width="700" />

<br>

<span style="color:white">There's no single way to build one. Different budgets, aesthetics, and use cases lead to very different setups. The only rule is to start with something that works, then iterate.</span>

<br>

## WHAT IS YOUR CYBERDECK FOR

<span style="color:white">Before building anything, decide what you want it to do.</span>

- write, take notes, journal
- SSH and code anywhere
- distraction-free machine
- small AI assistant or LLM pet
- a gaming console with an emulator
- your own e-ink reader
- media device: music, movies, reading
- portable travel setup

<span style="color:white">This choice matters more than the hardware. It defines everything that comes next.</span>

<br>

## LEVELS

<span style="color:white"><span style="color:#ff2777">**Beginner**</span>: off-the-shelf parts. Raspberry Pi or old laptop, small screen, keyboard, power bank. Everything is plugged together, maybe thrown into a box. Fast to build, no friction.</span>

<span style="color:white"><span style="color:#ff2777">**Intermediate**</span>: now you care about form. You integrate components, clean up cables, maybe add an internal battery or a custom case. It starts to feel like a real device.</span>

<span style="color:white"><span style="color:#ff2777">**Advanced**</span>: fully custom. Internal wiring, soldering, optimized layout, extra modules, switches, LEDs. Built for a specific use, not just assembled.</span>

<span style="color:white">You don't need to "reach" a level. You just move forward naturally.</span>

<br>
<br>
<br>

<img src="../assets_lab/deck.jpg" alt="cyberdeck setup" width="700" />

## WHAT YOU ACTUALLY NEED

<span style="color:white">Every cyberdeck comes down to the same core pieces:</span>

<ul style="color:white">
  <li>compute: Raspberry Pi, mini PC, old laptop</li>
  <li>screen: HDMI display or reused panel</li>
  <li>keyboard</li>
  <li>power: battery or power bank</li>
  <li>connections: cables, adapters, hub</li>
</ul>

<span style="color:white">That's it.</span>

<span style="color:white">You can build a first version for cheap, especially secondhand. Around <span style="color:#ff2777">**$100**</span> is enough to get something working.</span>

<span style="color:white">You can add so many accessories depending on what you want to do with it in the first place. It could be an <span style="color:#ff2777">**e-ink screen**</span>, a <span style="color:#ff2777">**touchscreen**</span>, a <span style="color:#ff2777">**solar panel**</span>, speakers, and much more.</span>

<br>

<img src="../assets_lab/case.jpg" alt="cyberdeck case" width="700" />

<br>

<span style="color:white">One of my favorite parts personally is choosing the <span style="color:#ff2777">**shell**</span>. I've seen many cool projects built with <span style="color:#ff2777">**upcycled cases**</span>. You can just go to a <span style="color:#ff2777">**thrift store**</span>, a <span style="color:#ff2777">**secondhand shop**</span>, or browse <span style="color:#ff2777">**eBay**</span> and have fun.</span>

<br>

## CHOOSE THE COMPUTE

<span style="color:white">I would recommend starting with a <span style="color:#ff2777">**raspberry pi 4**</span>. It lets you do quite a lot of things already, and for a first build it's probably what I would recommend most.</span>

<span style="color:white">After that, it really depends on what you want from the machine. I found this little chart, like some of the other images, on tiktok.</span>

<br>

<img src="../assets_lab/brain.jpg" alt="selecting the brain" width="700" />

<br>

<span style="color:white">This one will need to be powered too. So you can go for an <span style="color:#ff2777">**external battery**</span>, and you might even already have one at home. Just be careful about the <span style="color:#ff2777">**voltage**</span>. Otherwise you can keep it plugged in with cables, but I think that's annoying if you actually want to move the cyberdeck around.</span>

<br>

## USE AN LLM

<span style="color:white">IMO you can get through most of this with an <span style="color:#ff2777">**LLM in your pocket**</span>.</span>

<span style="color:white">You don't need to know everything in advance. You mostly need to know how to ask practical questions: what screen works with what board, how much power a setup draws, what cable or converter you need, how to mount parts, how to wire a switch, how to debug something that doesn't boot.</span>

<span style="color:white">Hardware gets much less intimidating once you treat it as a sequence of small concrete problems instead of one giant technical wall.</span>
